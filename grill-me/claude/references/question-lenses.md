# Selective question lenses

Use only a lens that could change the next decision, reveal a material gap, or help a stuck user. Adapt the wording to their context. Do not recite this list or require every lens.

| When it helps | Probe |
| --- | --- |
| The proposed task may solve the wrong problem | What prompted this? What outcome matters even if the proposed solution disappears? |
| Values or desired outcomes remain vague | What would improve for you or the affected people? Which tradeoff would you regret? |
| A constraint is hidden or possibly negotiable | Which limit is fixed, why, and who controls it? What changes if it relaxes? |
| There is only one apparent option | What is the strongest alternative, including doing less, waiting, or maintaining the current state? |
| Confidence exceeds the evidence | What needs to hold for this to work? Which claim would most change the choice if false? |
| The outcome depends on inputs or integrations | What content or data will be needed, where will it come from, and is it available? Which formats, examples, scale, and system connections could change feasibility or scope? |
| The plan depends on other people | Who is affected, what would they need, and which reaction is assumed rather than observed? |
| Scope is expanding | What is necessary for the next outcome? What can be consciously excluded or deferred? |
| Failure would be expensive | Imagine a plausible failure. What caused it, what early signal would appear, and what response is possible? |
| The choice may be hard to reverse | What is reversible? What is the smallest commitment that produces useful evidence? |
| The brief lacks rejection criteria | What would make you reject the result even if the listed requirements were met? |
| Taste or real-world behavior cannot be resolved verbally | What example, prototype, observation, or comparison could discriminate between the options? |
| Resources or maintenance may undermine the plan | Who sustains it? What happens if it takes longer or requires more effort than expected? |

Ask neutral questions when discovering intent. Where useful, offer a provisional option and invite correction, making clear what is assumed. An objection to the framing is a reason to reformulate the question, not repeat it more forcefully.
